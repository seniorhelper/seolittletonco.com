Provider pages

Patients pick a person, not a logo

For many patients, the first real decision is which doctor or dentist, not which practice. A strong provider page answers what they want to know before they book:

  • Name, credentials and board certifications
  • Education, residency and years in practice
  • Treatments and conditions they focus on
  • Languages spoken and which location they see patients at
  • A real, recent photo and a short personal note

Add Physician or Dentist structured data so machines connect each provider to the practice. When health content on your site is written or reviewed by a provider, say so with a byline and date. That is the kind of verifiable expertise Google and AI tools look for.

One profile or several?

Google allows individual practitioner listings in some cases, alongside the practice listing. Done right, it adds visibility. Done wrong, it splits reviews and confuses patients. Treatment and insurance pages

Answer the questions patients ask before they call

A single "Services" page listing twenty treatments ranks for none of them.

Treatment pages

One page per core treatment: who it is for, what happens at the visit, recovery, typical timelines and cost ranges where you can share them. For a dental office that might be implants, Invisalign, crowns, root canals and emergency visits. For a medical office, the conditions and procedures you most want to treat.

Insurance and payment pages

List the plans you accept, explain out-of-network billing, describe membership or financing options and say what a new patient visit costs without insurance. Patients search by plan name, and a clear answer saves your front desk time.

New patient path

Online booking or a short request form, directions and parking near your office, what to bring, and accessibility details. Less friction means more booked first visits.

AI-friendly answers

Short, direct answers to real questions near the top of each page, written in plain language. These are the passages AI assistants quote. See our approach to AI search for Littleton practices.

Reviews and HIPAA

Every reply is public. Write it that way.

Reviews matter in health care as much as anywhere. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 74% of consumers said they only care about reviews from the last three months, and 47% said they won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. A practice with a handful of old reviews looks inactive, even if the waiting room is full.

Source: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026.

Replies are where practices get into trouble. Confirming someone was a patient, mentioning a procedure or correcting their version of a visit can disclose protected health information. Keep every reply general. The same caution applies to positive reviews: a warm thank-you is fine, but naming the treatment the reviewer mentioned is not.

We set up a steady, compliant way to ask every patient, with no incentives, plus reply templates your team can trust. More on the process in our Littleton review management service.

Safe reply example

"Thank you for sharing this. We take every concern seriously and would welcome the chance to talk. Please call our office manager at your convenience."

No confirmation of a visit, no details of care, an invitation to take it offline.

FAQ

Dental and medical SEO in Littleton, answered

Can I reply to a negative patient review without violating HIPAA?

Yes, if you never confirm the person was a patient or mention anything about their care. Thank them for the feedback, state your general commitment to patients, and invite them to call the office directly. Even saying we are sorry your procedure went that way can confirm a patient relationship, so keep replies general.

Should each dentist or physician have their own page?

Yes. A page for each provider with credentials, training, specialties, languages and a real photo helps patients choose and gives Google and AI assistants verified facts to trust. Some providers can also have their own Google Business Profile listing when they meet Google's guidelines for practitioners.

Do insurance pages help a dental or medical practice rank?

They often do, because patients search for a dentist or doctor that takes their specific plan. A clear page listing the plans you accept, how out-of-network billing works and what a visit costs without insurance answers a real question and reduces front desk calls. Keep it current, since an outdated insurance list frustrates patients fast.

Is it allowed to offer patients a discount for leaving a review?

No. Google prohibits incentives for reviews, and offering them can also create problems under health care rules and state regulations. Ask every patient the same way, at the same point in their visit, and let them decide.

How can a Littleton practice compete with larger groups in Highlands Ranch and Lone Tree?

Win on specificity and proximity. Build deep pages for the treatments you most want, earn steady reviews from patients in Littleton, Columbine and Ken Caryl, and make your providers visible as real people. Large groups often have thin, templated location pages that a focused local practice can outperform.

What should a medical website avoid saying?

Avoid promising outcomes, using patient photos or stories without written consent, and making claims you cannot support with sources. Health content should be accurate, reviewed by a qualified provider and dated. Overstated claims hurt trust with patients and with search engines.
