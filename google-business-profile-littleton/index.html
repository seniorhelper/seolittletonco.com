Categories and services

The quiet setting that decides which searches you appear for

Your primary category tells Google what you are. A Littleton business choosing "Contractor" when it really does roofing, or "Doctor" when it is a pediatric practice, is asking to compete for vague searches while missing the specific ones that turn into calls.

We look at which categories the businesses already in the map pack for your top searches are using, then pick the most precise primary category that fits your real business. Secondary categories cover the other things you truly do. Services and their descriptions fill in the details, in the plain words customers use: "water heater replacement," not "residential hot water solutions."

Your business description deserves the same care. You get 750 characters, and the first line or two matter most. Say what you do, who you serve and where, in plain sentences: a line like "Family dental practice on West Bowles serving Littleton, Columbine and Ken Caryl" beats a paragraph of slogans. Leave out links, prices that change and claims like "best in Littleton," which Google can reject.

The same goes for attributes like wheelchair access, appointment links and online estimates. They are small, but they answer questions before a customer has to call, and they give Google and AI tools more facts to match against a search.

Retest after changes

Category changes can shift rankings within days, both up and down. We change one thing at a time, note the date and measure the map grid again so we know what helped.

Suspensions and verification

When the profile disappears overnight

Suspensions happen to honest businesses. A move across town, a name change, a burst of edits or a new manager can all trigger a review, and sometimes a competitor reports you. The worst response is panic: creating a second profile, deleting the first or changing ten things at once.

A good recovery process is calm and documented. Fix the likely cause, gather proof that your business is real at the place you claim, then submit one clean request and follow up patiently. Video verification is now common, so we help you plan a short clip showing your signage, your workspace and proof you operate there.

This is a big part of Google Business Profile management from Eye To Ad Media: preventing trouble before it starts and having a plan when it shows up anyway.

If your profile is suspended, stuck in verification or owned by someone who no longer works with you, tell us what happened and we will help you sort out the next step.

  1. Diagnose. Check the name, address type, recent edits and categories for anything that breaks the guidelines.
  2. Correct. Fix the issue on the profile and on your website so they match.
  3. Document. Business license, utility bill, signage photos and a verification video if requested.
  4. Appeal once. One complete reinstatement request, then patient follow-up.
  5. Protect. Set owner and manager roles correctly and monitor for unwanted edits.

FAQ

Google Business Profile questions from Littleton owners

Can I put Littleton in my Google Business Profile name to rank better?

Only if Littleton is part of your real business name, as it appears on your signage and paperwork. Adding keywords or city names to the profile name breaks Google's guidelines, competitors can report it, and it is a leading cause of suspensions. Put the city in your description, services and website instead.

My Google Business Profile got suspended. What should I do first?

Do not create a new profile, which usually makes things worse. Fix whatever likely triggered it, such as a keyword-stuffed name or an invalid address, then file a reinstatement request with proof like a business license, utility bill and photos of your signage. Keep a record of every step and response.

How many categories should my Google Business Profile have?

Pick one primary category that matches your main money service as exactly as possible, then add secondary categories only for services you really offer. More is not better. A Littleton dental office with ten loosely related categories often ranks worse than one with three precise ones.

How often should I post on my Google Business Profile?

Weekly is a good rhythm for most Littleton businesses, and more often around events or offers. Posts rarely move rankings much on their own, but they show an active business, answer questions before people call and give AI tools fresh facts to work with.

Who should own my Google Business Profile, me or my marketing company?

You should, always. Your business should be the primary owner and any agency, including us, should be added as a manager. If a past agency or web designer owns your profile, request ownership transfer now while things are calm, not during a crisis.

What hours should I show while Main Street or Santa Fe construction affects access?

Keep regular hours accurate and use special hours for any day you close or open late. Add a post or update explaining where to park and how to reach your door, and refresh it when conditions change. Customers forgive detours far more easily than a locked door that Google said was open.
