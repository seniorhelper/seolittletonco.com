Downtown · Merchant playbook

Main Street is changing. Keep your customers with you.

By Zach Wennstedt · Updated October 8, 2026 · 8 min read

Project Downtown will reshape historic Main Street over the coming years. Here is what is actually decided so far, and a marketing plan that keeps downtown merchants easy to find and easy to visit through every phase.

Direct answer: Downtown Littleton merchants can protect sales during Project Downtown by keeping Google Business Profile hours and access notes current, telling customers exactly how to park or arrive by light rail at the Littleton·Downtown station, building their own email and SMS lists, promoting events and Shop Local Rewards, and asking for fresh reviews. The Main Street concept plan was adopted in November 2024, about $32.6 million has been secured, and the project is in preliminary design.

Start with the facts

What the city has actually decided

Rumors travel fast on Main Street. Before planning your marketing, separate what is known from what is guessed. According to the city's Project Downtown page, here is where things stand.

  • The Main Street concept plan was adopted in November 2024.
  • About $32.6 million has been secured for Main Street improvements.
  • Main Street is in preliminary design as of 2026.
  • A separate Santa Fe and Mineral improvements project is under construction.

What is not known yet matters just as much. No Main Street construction start date has been announced, so anyone telling you a specific month is guessing. Plan for flexibility, not a fixed date.

Source: Let's Talk Littleton, Project Downtown: Main Street.

Why plan now

Preparation is cheap. Scrambling is not.

Every step in this playbook is useful today, construction or not. Merchants who build these habits during the design phase will be ready when schedules land, and will keep the customers others lose to confusion.

The core risk

Customers do not avoid construction. They avoid uncertainty.

When a street changes, the shops on it rarely lose customers because the work is noisy. They lose them because people are not sure the shop is open, not sure where to park, or not sure the effort is worth it. A shopper who has to guess usually chooses somewhere easier, often a chain in a big parking lot.

Your job is to remove every guess. That means clear, current, consistent information in the places people check before they leave home: Google, Apple Maps, your website, your social accounts and your own customer list. The rest of this playbook is about doing that without making it a second full-time job.

Move one

Treat your Google Business Profile like a front window

For many shoppers, your profile is the first and only thing they check. Keep it as current as the sign on your door.

Hours, always right

Update regular hours, holiday hours and any special hours the moment they change. Wrong hours are the fastest way to lose trust and a review.

Access in the description

Add a sentence about how to reach you, such as which cross street, which entrance or which nearby lot. Keep it short and update it when things change.

Weekly updates

Use profile updates to share new arrivals, event hours or "we're open, here's the easiest way in." Recent activity also signals that the business is alive.

Fresh photos

Post photos of your storefront as it looks now, including any temporary entrance. Customers recognize what they have seen.

Do the same on Apple Business Connect and Bing Places. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found Apple Maps' share of review usage rose from 14% to 27%, so a stale Apple listing reaches more shoppers than it used to. Our Google Business Profile service for Littleton businesses keeps all three in sync.

Source: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026.

Move two

Make getting here the easy part

Parking is the first question most downtown visitors ask, and the one most websites never answer. Write a short "Getting here" section on your site and pin it to your social profiles. Cover the nearest public parking, the walk time from it, and any accessible entrance.

Then lean on the light rail. The Littleton·Downtown station sits steps from the historic district, which gives you a message most suburban retailers cannot use. "Take light rail to Littleton·Downtown and walk two blocks" is a real reason to visit on a busy weekend.

When construction phases begin, update this section first. A single page you can edit in five minutes is better than a dozen scattered posts that go stale.

Your "Getting here" block

  • Nearest public parking and walk time
  • Light rail: Littleton·Downtown station
  • Bike racks and accessible entrance
  • Current temporary access notes, dated
  • A map link that drops a pin on your door

Move three

Own your audience with email and SMS

Platforms change their rules. Your customer list is the one channel nobody can take away.

  1. Pick one tool. Any reputable email platform works. Add SMS only if you will actually use it for timely notes like event hours or a flash sale.
  2. Collect at every touchpoint. A sign at the register, a line on receipts, a simple form on your website. Ask permission clearly. Never add people who did not opt in.
  3. Give a reason to stay. Early access to events, first look at new stock, a members-only offer during slower weeks.
  4. Send on a rhythm. Monthly is enough for most shops. Add a short note whenever access changes.
  5. Link back to your site. Every message should point to a page with current hours and the getting-here details.

That last step only pays off if the page converts. Unclear buttons, buried hours and forms that break on phones quietly cost downtown merchants sales. These conversion mistakes we see on local business websites are worth checking against your own.

Move four

Use events and Shop Local Rewards as reasons to come back

Ride the big events

Western Welcome Week and the Candlelight Walk bring crowds downtown. Publish your event hours and specials on your profile and site a few weeks ahead.

Promote Shop Local Rewards

The city's program gives shoppers 5% back through the Open Rewards app at participating businesses. If you take part, say so on your door, site and profile.

Create small moments

A first-Friday tasting, a kids' craft hour, a local maker pop-up. Small, repeatable events give customers a reason to visit even when streets are messy.

Source: City of Littleton, Shop Local Rewards. Check the city's page for the current program details.

Restaurants have extra levers, such as menus, reservations and delivery listings. If you run one, it is worth reading up on restaurant marketing built around those channels.

Move five

Keep reviews coming, and answer every one

Reviews are where customers check whether a place is still worth the trip. BrightLocal found 74% of consumers only care about reviews from the last three months. During a disruptive stretch, fresh reviews that say "worth the walk" or "easy parking around the corner" do your marketing for you.

Ask happy customers in person, then follow up with a direct link. Reply to every review, including the grumpy ones about construction. A calm reply that explains the easiest way in helps the next reader more than the original complaint hurts.

Reply template

"Thanks for making the trip. The easiest way in right now is the side entrance off the alley, and the public lot is a two-minute walk. See you soon."

Move six

Tell the story, not just the logistics

Customers are curious about the changes on Main Street, and many of them care about downtown's future as much as you do. Use that. A short note on your website or in your newsletter about what the new street could mean for visitors turns a disruption into a shared project.

Keep it factual and point people to the city's page for official details. Avoid repeating rumors about dates or closures. Your job is to be the calm, reliable voice that says "we're here, here's how to reach us, and here's where to find the real updates."

Write a page on your site that answers the questions customers ask you at the counter. That same page helps search engines and AI tools describe your business accurately during the transition.

Questions to answer on your site

  • Are you open during the work?
  • Where is the closest parking right now?
  • Which entrance should I use?
  • Can I order ahead or pick up curbside?
  • Where do I find official project updates?

The long view

Built for the street that comes next

Projects like this are designed to make downtown a better place to visit for decades. The merchants who benefit most will be the ones who kept their customers informed and engaged through the transition, then had a list, a reputation and a search presence ready when the new street opened.

None of that requires a big budget. It requires current information, a few honest habits and the discipline to keep them going. Start this month, while Main Street is still in design.

FAQ

Questions Littleton owners ask

When will Main Street construction start in downtown Littleton?

The city has not announced a construction start date for Main Street. As of 2026 the project is in preliminary design. Follow the official Project Downtown page on Let's Talk Littleton for schedule updates, and plan your marketing so you can adapt quickly when dates are published.

Should I mark my business as temporarily closed on Google during nearby construction?

Only if you are actually closed. Marking a business temporarily closed when it is open can cost you calls and visits. If you are open with changed access, keep the profile open, update hours if needed, and post a short update explaining how to reach your door.

How can a downtown shop collect customer emails without being pushy?

Offer something useful in return, such as early notice of events, a members-only sale or a monthly note about what is new. Keep the signup short at the register and on your website, and never add people without permission. A small list of engaged locals is worth more than a large list of strangers.

Is it worth giving feedback on the Main Street design as a business owner?

Yes. Public input shapes details like loading zones, sidewalk space and signage that directly affect your business. Use the city's engagement page to comment, and share what customers tell you about parking and access so the feedback reflects real shopping habits.
