A 20-minute Search Console check for Littleton owners

By Zach Wennstedt · Published October 9, 2026 · 6 min read

Google Search Console is free, it comes straight from Google, and most small business owners never open it. Here is a short monthly routine that tells you whether Littleton customers are finding you and what to fix first.

Direct answer: Once a month, open Google Search Console and check four things: the Performance report for clicks and the searches that bring people in, the Pages report for pages Google cannot index, the Sitemaps report for errors, and any security or manual action warnings. Compare against the previous period, write down what changed, and fix indexing problems before chasing new keywords.

Why bother

The one free report that comes straight from Google

Most marketing reports are someone's interpretation. Search Console is Google telling you, in its own numbers, which searches showed your website, how often people clicked, and which of your pages it could not add to its index. For a Littleton business that depends on local search, that is the closest thing to a straight answer you will get.

You do not need to live in it. A short routine once a month catches most problems early, long before they show up as a quiet phone. If you have not set it up yet, add your site as a Domain property and verify it with a DNS record at your domain registrar. That covers every version of your address, with and without www.

Minutes 1 to 8

Step 1: the Performance report

Open Performance, then Search results. Set the date range to the last 28 days and use the Compare option against the previous period. You will see four numbers: total clicks, total impressions, average click-through rate and average position.

Clicks are the number that matters most, because clicks are people. Impressions tell you how often you showed up at all. A rise in impressions with flat clicks usually means you are appearing for more searches but not yet high enough, or your title is not convincing anyone.

Next, click the Queries tab and look for searches that mention Littleton, nearby neighborhoods such as Columbine or Ken Caryl, or your service plus "near me." Then click Pages to see which pages earn those clicks. Write down the top five of each in a simple spreadsheet so next month has something to compare against.

Quick wins to look for

What the numbers usually point to

  • High impressions, low clicks. Rewrite that page's title and meta description so they match what people searched.
  • Position 8 to 20. These pages are close. Adding useful detail and a few internal links often helps.
  • Your business name dropping. Branded searches falling can point to a listing or reputation problem, not SEO.

Minutes 9 to 15

Step 2: the Pages report

Under Indexing, open Pages. The chart splits your URLs into indexed and not indexed, and the table below lists the reasons. Some reasons are normal, such as pages you deliberately marked noindex or alternate versions with a proper canonical tag. Others need attention:

  • Not found (404) on a page that used to exist. Add a 301 redirect to the closest live page.
  • Excluded by noindex tag on a page you want found. This often slips through after a website update.
  • Crawled, currently not indexed. Google looked and passed. The page is usually thin or too similar to another one, so make it more useful.
  • Server error (5xx). Tell whoever hosts your site. Repeated errors make Google crawl less often.

When you fix something, use Validate fix so Google rechecks those URLs. For a single important page, paste its address into the URL Inspection bar at the top and request indexing.

Minutes 16 to 20

Step 3: sitemaps, warnings and a note for next month

Open Sitemaps and confirm your sitemap shows a status of Success with a recent read date. If you added pages this month, they should be in it. Then glance at Security and Manual actions. On a healthy site both say no issues detected, and that is all you need to see.

Finish by writing three lines in your spreadsheet: clicks this month, the biggest change you noticed, and the one thing you will fix before next month. That habit matters more than any single report, because it turns Search Console from a dashboard you ignore into a list of small, finished jobs.

Search Console does not show your Google Business Profile results, which is where many Littleton map-pack calls start. FAQ

Questions Littleton owners ask

Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. Search Console is a free service from Google. You verify that you own the website, usually with a DNS record or a small file or meta tag, and Google starts showing you data about how your site appears in search.

What is the difference between Search Console and Google Analytics?

Search Console shows what happens before the click: which searches showed your pages, how often, and how many people clicked. Google Analytics shows what visitors do after they arrive. Most businesses benefit from having both.

Why does Search Console show fewer clicks than my analytics?

The two tools count differently. Search Console only counts clicks from Google Search, and it leaves out some rare queries for privacy. Analytics counts visits from every source. Look at the trend in each tool rather than expecting the numbers to match.

How often should a small business check Search Console?

Once a month is enough for most small businesses, plus a quick look after any website change, redesign or move. Google also emails the verified owner when it finds a serious problem, so make sure that email goes to an inbox someone reads.
