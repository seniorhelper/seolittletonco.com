The job of a website

What should a Littleton small business website do in 2027?

A website has one job before any other: convince the person who just found you on Google, Apple Maps or an AI assistant that you are the safe choice, and make the next step obvious. Pretty matters less than people think. Clarity matters more.

Picture a homeowner in Columbine at 9 p.m. with a leaking water heater, or a parent near Aspen Grove comparing two pediatric dentists on a phone in the car-pool line. Neither will read a long mission statement. They scan for three things: do you do this, do you serve my area, and how do I reach you right now. A site that answers those in the first screen wins more of those visitors than a beautiful one that hides them.

The second job is to feed search. Google, Bing and the AI tools need pages they can read, understand and trust. That means clean code, descriptive headings, structured data, and one focused page for each service you want to be found for.

First-screen checklist

  • What you do, in plain words
  • Where you serve, named specifically
  • A tap-to-call button and a short form
  • One proof point: reviews, years, credentials
  • Loads quickly on a phone signal, not just Wi-Fi

Google also indexes the mobile version of a site first, so a page that looks fine on a desktop monitor but crawls on a phone is judged by the slow version. The usual culprits are oversized photos, heavy page builders, sliders, a pile of plugins and third-party scripts that load before the content. Custom code avoids most of those by design. Where we do use outside tools, such as booking widgets or chat, we load them after the page is usable.

Site structure

Which pages should a Littleton business website have?

Most small business sites need fewer pages than owners expect, but the right ones. A typical structure looks like this:

  • Home. Who you are, what you do, where, and the next step.
  • One page per core service. “Dental implants” and “teeth cleaning” are separate searches, so they deserve separate pages.
  • Service area. The parts of the Littleton area you truly cover, written accurately.
  • About and team. Real names, real photos, credentials and history.
  • Reviews or results. Proof in your customers’ words.
  • Contact. Phone, form, hours, a map and parking notes.

Location wording deserves care in Littleton. Columbine and Ken Caryl are unincorporated Jefferson County, Roxborough sits further south, and several “Littleton” mailing addresses fall outside city limits. Accessibility

Does a Littleton business website need to be accessible?

It should be. The U.S. Department of Justice’s 2022 guidance on web accessibility states that businesses open to the public must provide equal access to people with disabilities, including online. The most widely used technical standard is WCAG, the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines; version 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation in October 2023.

In practice, accessible design is also better design: readable contrast, text that scales, buttons large enough to tap, forms with clear labels, image descriptions, a page that works with a keyboard and no content that flashes. We build to WCAG 2.2 AA techniques and check with automated and manual tests. No tool or vendor can certify that a site is free of every barrier, and anyone who promises that is overselling, but a careful build removes the common ones.

Quick self-check

Open your site on a phone outdoors in daylight. Can you read every line? Tap every button without zooming? Fill the form with one thumb? If not, your customers are struggling too.

How long does it take to build a new website?

A focused small business site usually takes a few weeks from kickoff to launch. The biggest variable is content: photos, service details and approvals from your side. Larger sites with booking, e-commerce or many location pages take longer, and we give you a written timeline before work starts.

Will a new website hurt my Google rankings?

It can if a redesign changes URLs without redirects, drops pages that were bringing traffic or removes content. We map every existing URL, set permanent redirects, keep what is working and check Google Search Console after launch so rankings are protected through the move.

Do I need a custom-coded site or is a website builder enough?

A builder can be fine for a brand-new business on a tight budget. Custom-coded sites tend to load faster, give full control over structured data and page layout, and avoid plugin bloat. If organic search is a main source of customers, custom code usually pays for itself over time.

Should my Littleton website have pages for each neighborhood?

Only where you really serve customers and have something specific to say. A page for Ken Caryl or Aspen Grove that explains how you serve that area, with real photos and reviews from there, can help. Near-duplicate pages with only the place name swapped can hurt.
